# Phase 348: the phone, polished, your check

Six rows, on your own iPhone and this Mac, after the Mac release and phone build 12, which carry Phase 348, are both
installed. It takes about fifteen minutes. Your Mac Pro should be one of this Mac's machines, with a session running
there.

| # | Do this | You should see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | On the Mac, open Settings, then Phone. Read the block above Allow, then press Allow once. | Above Allow, two lines: `New: remove sessions from your phone` with `An ended session moves to Past Sessions on this Mac, where you can restore it.`, and `New: paste into sessions from your phone` with `It lands as a paste at this Mac does, with no Return added.` One press of Allow and nothing else waits. |
| 2 | On the phone, in Settings, press Pair another Mac and pair a Mac (or Unpair this iPhone and pair this Mac again). Then quit the app and open it again. | The moment the pairing finishes, the phone is on that Mac's Sessions tab, not Needs input. After the relaunch it opens on Needs input, as before. At the foot of both lists you read `read <time>` alone: the sentence about waits first seen after your Mac wakes is gone. (The landed checklists for 316 and 316.6 still say that sentence is there; it is not.) |
| 3 | Look at Settings, then at the top of Needs input, Sessions and Macs. On Sessions press Select, then Cancel, then the filter, then "+". | Settings is grouped like the iPhone's own Settings: This Mac, Alerts, Pair another Mac, then Unpair this iPhone in a group of its own, See a sample, About, the same rows in the same order, small grey symbols and no coloured tiles. Each tab has the iPhone's own large title, which shrinks into the bar when you scroll. On Sessions, Select and the filter sit at the top left of the bar and the "+" alone at its top right, a little shorter to press than before, by your choice (iOS draws every top-bar button 36 pt tall); All, Active and Ended keep their full width under the title. Each opens what it opened before. |
| 4 | End a session on this Mac from the phone (Catch Me Up's End, with Face ID). When it reads ended, press Remove where End was, and confirm. Then look at Past Sessions on the Mac and restore it there. | Remove is where End was, in the Mac's own words: `Remove '<name>'?` and `It moves to Past Sessions and you can restore it from there.` No Face ID or passcode is asked for Remove. The phone goes back to the list and the session is gone from it, under All too. On the Mac it is in Past Sessions, and Restore brings its conversation back. End these and End still ask Face ID, as before. |
| 5 | Copy a line of text in Notes or Safari. Open a running session on this Mac in the phone's Terminal. Press and hold on the terminal, then press Paste; do that five times. With a hardware keyboard, press Command-V; without one, pinch open with three fingers. Then open a session running on your Mac Pro and press and hold there. | The long-press menu shows Copy and Paste. Each of the three ways pastes the text into the session as a paste at the desk does, with no Return pressed after it, and iOS never asks to allow pasting. Every one of the five Pastes pastes, once: in the Simulator one press in ten read nothing from the clipboard and pasted nothing, with no line, and this is where a phone says whether that happens. If the first Command-V after you come back from Notes does nothing, press it once more: that is a known limit, and nothing is pasted twice. A clipboard that is only a Tortie pairing code is refused on the phone with a line, and nothing is sent; a code inside other text is pasted like any text. On the Mac Pro's session the menu shows Copy alone: a paste reaches sessions on this Mac only. |
| 6 | On the Mac, in a shell session, run `echo` with an argument longer than the terminal is wide, so the output wraps. On the phone, open that session's Terminal, select the wrapped output, Copy it, and paste it into Notes. | It comes back as one line, exactly as it was printed. The stated exceptions: a line the terminal wrapped exactly at a space, and a command still being typed at the prompt, each come back broken as they do today; agents break their own lines; and, rarely, a long line a full-screen program redrew in place, or one that wrapped into a region the program then scrolled, comes back joined to the line after it, as tmux's own copy joins them. |

If anything in the right-hand column is not what you see, write down which row and which part, and stop there.
